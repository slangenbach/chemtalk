Executive Summary 🎯

One CoVA, different voice experiences — matching the user’s working environment.

  • Start simple: Extend CoVA with turn-based voice interaction.
  • Test where it gets difficult: Jargon, noise, PPE, and connectivity.
  • Compare deliberately: Hybrid and full-duplex where they improve task completion.
  • About 1 minute.
  • Recommend a baseline first, then compare alternatives using the same tasks.
  • Reuse CoVA’s knowledge and permissions.
  • We are proposing a validation programme, not assuming a single voice experience suits every role.

Problem Statement & Assumptions 🤔💭

What is the real challenge? 🤔

Enable blue-collar workers to interact with CoVA safely, reliably, and hands-free in constrained environments.

  • Status quo: CoVA serves white-collar staff well via text chat.
  • Problem: Plant operators, lab technicians, and drivers must stop work, defer questions, or skip queries entirely.
  • Goal: Build robust voice interfaces tailored to each environment’s physical and technical constraints.
  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • Emphasize the plural in interfaces. Hands-free is the goal; a glove-compatible PTT control may be the practical first step.

Managing Expectations 💭

Assumptions

  • CoVA provides identity, permissions, message history, knowledge access, and text responses.
  • CoVA’s stack (Angular / TypeScript / AWS) is the fundament: PWA first, a native app reserved for environments that demand it.
  • English first, using company-managed pilot devices.
  • Stakeholder support (top management, plant, worker council, etc.) and budget for a representative pilot are a given.

Constraints

  • Device use and EX requirements vary by area.
  • Noise and PPE affect speaking and hearing.
  • Connectivity ranges from good to absent.
  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • English is a technical starting point, not evidence of suitability for local-language users.
  • Use explicit PTT/session activation initially
  • Full-duplex does not require an always-listening wake word.

Approach: Vision, Value & Options 🛠💰

Vision: What do we want to achieve? 🛠

A worker speaks a question and hears a useful answer quickly — without removing PPE or reaching for a keyboard, when the task permits it.

Short answers. Clear recording status. Easy repeat and stop.

  • About 1 minute.
  • Useful means: understandable, relevant, and usable in the current task.
  • Why “when the task permits it”? Safety always takes precedence.
  • A spoken wall of text is not a good voice interface; offer a short answer followed by more detail on request, with references available on screen.

Business Value: Where Does Voice Help? 💰

Persona Potential use case Value to measure
Lab technician Retrieve a method or reference Fewer trips to a terminal; faster lookup
Maintenance technician Retrieve troubleshooting knowledge Less interruption; faster access to context
Driver Handle task queries while driving between sites Access when typing is impractical

Prioritize by frequency × friction removed × feasibility × value generated.

  • About 1 minute.
  • Choose a frequent, bounded information-retrieval task with usable source material and permitted device use.

Business Value: Make It Measurable 📐

What improves when CoVA becomes accessible by voice?

Example: 100 users × 3 queries/day × 1 min saved = ~1,300 hrs/year (assuming 253 working days)

  • Direct benefit: Hands-busy lookup without stopping tasks or walking to terminals.
  • Strategic lever: Faster anomaly resolution avoids costly line stoppages.
  • About 1 minute.
  • Capacity released is not automatically cash savings.
  • MTTR and first-time-fix rate are downstream measures for maintenance expansion, not universal KPIs or benefits attributable to voice without evidence

KPIs: Are We on Track? 📊

Measure Definition
Adoption Weekly active users / eligible pilot users
Task success Voice queries answered usefully without retry or fallback to another channel
Latency p50/p95: end of speech → first useful spoken response
Domain accuracy Correct chemicals, equipment IDs, numbers, and units

Proposed pilot latency target: p95 ≤ 3 seconds in agreed test conditions.

  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • Latency budget justification (p95 ≤ 3s): Human conversational psychology says, if latency exceeds 2–3s, users assume the system failed and press PTT again, causing duplicate queries.
  • Evaluate meaningful response latency, not a filler phrase such as ‘Let me check’.
  • Track return usage, clarification, retries, mode changes, and user feedback as diagnostic measures.
  • Task success is per voice query, measured from correlated voice/text session logs; clarifications are not retries. Silent failure — an unusable answer accepted or a question deferred — is the blind spot, so read it together with domain accuracy and the weekly user review. Reading on-screen citations is not a fallback.

Options: How Do We Get There? 🔀

Option Interaction and processing What do we learn?
KISS PTT → cloud STT → CoVA → TTS Can voice deliver value quickly?
Hybrid Local STT/TTS + CoVA over a text connection Does on-device speech — bandwidth, cost, offline tolerance — pay off?
Full-duplex Streaming speech-to-speech + CoVA integration Does interruptible dialogue — follow-ups, no PTT — improve task completion while preserving grounded answers?

Processing location and conversation style are separate decisions.

  • About 2 minutes.
  • STT means speech-to-text; TTS means text-to-speech; PTT means push-to-talk.
  • Cloud Transcribe/Polly are the turn-based baseline candidates.
  • Local models such as Whisper are candidates to benchmark, not a settled mobile solution.
  • AWS Nova 2 Sonic is the full-duplex candidate.
  • Hybrid can be an environment-specific mode rather than an automatic fallback on every latency spike.
  • Full-duplex uniquely tests turn-taking/interruption and grounding fidelity through the tool bridge; session economics of persistent streaming are the second thing to learn.

Solutions: Architecture & Design 👩🏻‍🔧

Architecture: KISS (Cloud Speech) ☁️

  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • Reuses CoVA’s text path; speech is the only addition.
  • AWS building blocks: PWA on S3 + CloudFront; Voice API on API Gateway + Lambda; managed speech on Amazon Transcribe and Polly.
  • Implementation note: API Gateway/Lambda can implement bounded turn-based requests; establish audio/request limits and use an asynchronous path if processing exceeds the chosen integration timeout.
  • Device credentials authenticate access, but the backend must validate the user session and enforce permissions.

Architecture: Hybrid (On-Device Speech) 📱

Difference to KISS: speech runs on-device, only text crosses the network, and no extra voice backend is needed.

  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • Same CoVA integration; only the speech runtime moves on-device.
  • No extra voice backend: the PWA calls CoVA’s existing API directly — the same path CoVA’s own Angular frontend uses; no AWS speech services are billed in this mode.
  • On-device TTS is an option: small neural TTS models such as Kokoro (82M params) or Piper (VITS) run in the browser via WebAssembly/WebGPU (e.g. sherpa-onnx or transformers.js), fully offline.
  • Trade-off: local TTS voice quality and language coverage are below cloud Polly, and the model bundle adds tens of MB to the PWA — benchmark before committing.

Architecture: Full-duplex 💬

  • About 2 minutes.
  • Full-duplex adds a conversational model around CoVA.
  • Use InvokeModelWithBidirectionalStream and adapt the AWS TypeScript sample [1].
  • Interruption handling: Nova Sonic natively handles interruptions. When the worker speaks while audio is playing, new audio events sent via WebSocket trigger an interruption event from Sonic back to the Session Service, causing the UI to immediately cancel audio playback.
  • The bidirectional stream is two concurrent edges: worker audio upstream, model audio and events downstream. Tool calls originate from the model (toolUse); the session service executes them and returns the result (toolResult).

Trade-offs: Make it fit the Environment ⚖️

Factor KISS Hybrid Full-duplex
Integration Reuse CoVA text path Add local speech runtime Add streaming and tool bridge
Network Audio upload/download Mostly text Continuous audio stream
Device load Capture and playback Plus local inference Capture and playback
Conversation Explicit turns Explicit turns Turn-taking and interruption

Accuracy, latency, battery, and cost: benchmark rather than assume.

  • About 1 minute.
  • Local STT is not inherently less accurate, and cloud STT is not automatically best for chemical jargon.
  • Results depend on model, vocabulary support, microphone, accent, and noise.
  • Local battery cost depends on actual duty cycle, not continuous inference for an entire shift.
  • Local model distribution can be separate from app updates.
  • Compare candidate runtimes on the supported Android/iOS devices before choosing one.

Dealing with Chemical Jargon 🧪

  • Curated test set: Chemical formulas, trade names, equipment IDs, and units.
  • Custom vocabulary: Domain biasing on STT where supported (e.g. Transcribe).
  • Knowledge grounding: CoVA RAG context to disambiguate terms and prevent hallucinations.
  • Audio verification: Validate phonetic clarity and fidelity of synthesized answers.
  • About 1 minute.
  • Recognition and knowledge grounding solve different problems: retrieving the right document cannot reliably fix an incorrectly recognized chemical name.
  • Amazon Transcribe supports custom vocabularies [2]; do not claim the same API exists for Sonic.
  • Use recordings with representative accents, noise, and PPE, plus held-out evaluation data.

Handling Noise & PPE 🎧

  • Input: Suitable, directional microphone and noise suppression.
  • Output: Intelligible speech with required hearing protection.
  • Controls: Glove-compatible activation, repeat/stop, clear recording status.
  • Validation: Actual phone + headset + PPE, during representative tasks.
  • About 1 minute.
  • Hardware is part of the solution, not an accessory.
  • What does “intelligible” mean here? Audible ≠ intelligible. High plant noise combined with hearing protection (earplugs/ear defenders attenuating 25–30 dB) turns soft consonants into muffled sound. If CoVA says “fifteen” and the worker hears “fifty”, or confuses “sulfate” with “sulfite”, it creates severe safety hazards.
  • A headset’s PTT button may not be exposed to browser code -> validate
  • If the PWA cannot support the required hardware interaction, move that persona to a native app

Connectivity: Weak Is Not Offline 📶

  • Good connection: Test cloud speech and full-duplex.
  • Low bandwidth: Local STT/TTS may keep text-based access usable.
  • No connection: Clearly indicate that live CoVA is unavailable.

Change modes explicitly: Avoid silently losing or replaying a question.

  • About 1 minute.
  • Measure connection setup, packet loss, drops, and reconnection, not just bandwidth.
  • A fallback based on one two-second delay is too simplistic; use observed network behavior and visible user control.
  • Cancel stale requests and prevent duplicate turns after retry.
  • Text display is an option only when eyes and equipment permit it.
  • A limited cache of approved information would be a separate versioned capability
  • Geolocation may suggest a mode but is not proof of device permission and should not automatically start recording.

Deployment, Rollout & Monitoring 🚀👷🏻

Deployment: Build on the Existing Stack 🚀

  • Frontend: Angular voice UI / PWA, delivered via S3 + CloudFront.
  • Turn-based API: API Gateway + Lambda; Transcribe/Polly and CoVA.
  • Full-duplex: Persistent TypeScript-based voice session service; AWS Nova 2 Sonic (or comparable model) via Bedrock.
  • Delivery: AWS CDK + GitHub Actions; pilot feature flags; canary rollout.

Hosting candidates for the full-duplex session service:

  • AWS Lambda MicroVMs: Dedicated WebSocket endpoint per session, fast snapshot start, suspends when idle (ideal for intermittent pilot queries; zero idle compute).
  • AWS ECS / Fargate: Shared container pool behind an ALB (ideal for production scale with high, sustained concurrency).
  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • Propose testing MicroVMs first for the intermittent pilot, subject to region availability and IaC support, rather than asserting they are faster or cheaper. A backend session broker authenticates the user, launches a MicroVM, and returns its endpoint plus a short-lived port-scoped JWE token. Browser WebSockets can pass MicroVM authentication via subprotocols [3]. Keep them running during active audio
  • Sonic has a separate eight-minute connection limit and renewal pattern [5].
  • Fargate may be more efficient when many sessions share warm tasks.

Rollout: Get to the Next Phase 👷🏻

Phase What we validate Exit evidence
Experiment Jargon, devices, and network profiles Known failures understood; quality gates agreed
Prototype One frequent, bounded use case Usable answers, acceptable latency, workflow benefit
Expansion Noise, PPE, connectivity, equipment Target setup works during representative tasks
Scale More sites, personas, and languages Repeatable quality, support, and economics

Proposed evaluation window: 6–12 weeks, not a promise of global production.

  • About 2 minutes.
  • Recruit representative users with operations, domain experts, and relevant local stakeholders.
  • Give users a short onboarding session and a clear feedback/support route.
  • Start with a permitted environment and a bounded use case, not an arbitrary headcount.
  • The expansion phase is the client decision gate: stay on the PWA where screens are active; plan a native track where PTT hardware, background use, or on-device speech demand it.

Monitoring: What Breaks? 🔎

  • Operations: Latency, failed sessions, connection drops, cost per successful task.
  • Quality: Domain errors, clarification, and task failure by mode and environment.
  • Feedback: Weekly review with users and domain experts.
  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • CloudWatch dashboards and correlated session traces across voice and CoVA. Separate capture, transcription, retrieval/answer, streaming, and playback failures.
  • Alert on service degradation and disable a problematic mode if needed.
  • Track model, vocabulary, prompt, device, and client versions to identify regressions.
  • Do not interpret every clarification as failure; the correct question can prevent a wrong answer.
  • Use selected, authorized diagnostic recordings with defined retention rather than logging all audio by default.

Limitations & Recommendation 🧐📝

Limitations 🧐

  • No connectivity: No (live) CoVA answer.
  • EX-certified equipment is costly.
  • Some environments may never support an approved, usable voice setup.
  • English-first validates the technology, not global adoption.
  • About 1 minute.
  • Equipment cost, battery life, model quality, and operational acceptance remain empirical questions.
  • CoVA is an information-access channel; it is not a substitute for required procedures or emergency systems.
  • Transition: given these limits, here is the next decision to make.

Recommendation: One CoVA, with voice modes that fit the job 📝

  1. Build a KISS baseline on the existing CoVA stack.
  2. Validate jargon, audio usability, and workflow value with representative users.
  3. Compare hybrid and full-duplex against the same tasks over 6–12 weeks.
  4. Expand by environment when the evidence supports it.
  • About 1.5 minutes.
  • Ask for a bounded pilot with an operational partner and access to representative tasks, devices, and source material.
  • The outcome is evidence for which modes and equipment to productionize, with an owner and support path.
  • We can keep one shared CoVA integration and evaluation framework while supporting multiple voice experiences.
  • Do not promise convergence to one mode or operating every option indefinitely.

Q&A

Thank you 🙏🏻

Likely questions & panel challenges:

  • Why Nova 2 Sonic vs. OpenAI Realtime / Gemini Live? Data residency, existing AWS CDK stack, IAM perimeter, and enterprise Bedrock tenant. Keeps data strictly inside Covestro. The session relay architecture isolates the model provider—if Sonic underperforms, we can swap backends without frontend changes.
  • PWA vs. Native Mobile? PWA first: it reuses CoVA’s stack and validates voice-to-CoVA fastest; the lab pilot uses active screens. Native is the planned expansion path where PWA limits bite — hardware PTT (Web Bluetooth absent on iOS), locked-screen/background capture, reliable local model caching — with MDM distribution removing store friction. Agents lower the native code cost, but the two-platform validation matrix is the real price; let evidence gate that investment.
  • RAG tool latency in full-duplex (Sonic waiting for CoVA)? Sonic supports conversational prosody, but artificial filler phrases annoy operators. The session service sends an audible earcon/status cue so the user knows CoVA is retrieving verified facts, avoiding ungrounded speech.
  • High EX headset costs vs. small pilot scale? In chemical operations, ROI is driven by avoiding unplanned downtime (€50k–€100k+ per stoppage) and compliance errors, not typing speed. Preventing one incident pays for the program.
  • Authentication without typing? Reuse CoVA/Entra sign-in on managed devices, within session policy. MDM alone does not authenticate a person. A MicroVM token is a separate endpoint credential.
  • Why not local STT everywhere? Benchmark it; feasibility, duty-cycle battery use, jargon, and supported device runtimes determine whether it wins.
  • On-device TTS quality vs. Polly? Kokoro/Piper are below cloud voice quality; A/B them in the pilot rather than assume hybrid wins. Neuphonic’s NeuTTS [7] shows rapid improvement (LM+codec architecture, GGUF, real-time on mid-range phones) and even has a German Nano model — relevant for the English-first limitation — but it is native-first (Python/llama.cpp); a PWA path would require WASM/ONNX packaging. Its voice-cloning capability is a worker-council/compliance question, not a technical detail.
  • Why MicroVMs? Dedicated WebSocket ingress and on-demand lifecycle are useful for intermittent sessions. Compare with pooled Fargate capacity; do not assume lower cost or latency.
  • What happens after a connection drop? Show status, cancel stale turns, reconnect and renew the Sonic stream, and restore context explicitly. Do not silently replay a question.
  • What if the answer is wrong? Keep CoVA’s grounding and authorization, confirm ambiguous entities, and evaluate spoken output separately. Grounding is not a guarantee against hallucination.
  • Make or buy? Use managed speech services and approved commercial equipment. Build the voice-to-CoVA adapter, user experience, and evaluation. Broader platform replacement is unnecessary for this scope.
  • Cost at scale? Measure speech duration, CoVA/tool inference, concurrency, hosting, snapshots/networking, equipment, and support. Estimate cost per successful task with the selected region’s current prices.
