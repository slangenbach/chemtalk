Reality Check 💁🏻‍♂️

How is voice mode working today?

  • Office workers: Easy access — think ChatGPT voice mode 👩🏻‍💼

  • Lab technicians: Constrained — gloves, PPE, and protocols 👨🏻‍🔬

Executive Summary 🎯

One CoVA, different voice experiences — matched to the users working environment.

  • Start simple: Extend CoVA with turn-based voice interaction.
  • Test where it gets difficult: Jargon, noise, PPE, and connectivity.
  • Compare deliberately: Hybrid and full-duplex where they improve task completion.

About 1 minute. Recommend a baseline first, then compare alternatives using the same tasks. Reuse CoVA’s knowledge and permissions. We are proposing a validation programme, not assuming a single voice experience suits every role.

Problem Statement & Assumptions 🤔💭

What is the real challenge? 🤔

Enable deskless workers to interact with CoVA safely, reliably, and hands-free in constrained environments.

  • Status quo: CoVA serves office staff well via text chat.
  • Problem: Plant operators, lab techs, and drivers must stop work, defer questions, or skip queries entirely.
  • Goal: Build robust voice interfaces tailored to each environment’s physical and technical constraints.

About 1.5 minutes. Emphasize the plural in interfaces. Hands-free is the goal; a glove-compatible PTT control may be the practical first step. The case study requires spoken input and spoken or readable answers with conversational latency. The scope is access to the existing assistant, not replacing its backend or adding autonomous plant control.

Managing Expectations 💭

Assumptions

  • CoVA provides identity, permissions, knowledge access, and text responses.
  • We use the Angular / TypeScript / AWS the fundament.
  • English first, using company-managed pilot devices.
  • Stakeholder support and budget for a representative pilot exist.

Constraints

  • Device use and EX requirements vary by area.
  • Noise and PPE affect speaking and hearing.
  • Connectivity ranges from good to absent.
  • Activation must work with the user’s task and equipment.

About 1.5 minutes. Assumptions come from context.md; pilot support and equipment budget need validation. Confirm the CoVA integration contract rather than assuming an existing public REST API. English is a technical starting point, not evidence of suitability for local-language users. Use explicit PTT/session activation initially; full-duplex does not require an always-listening wake word.

Approach: Vision, Value & Options 🛠💰

Vision: What do we want to achieve? 🛠

A worker speaks a question and hears a useful answer quickly — without removing PPE or reaching for a keyboard, when the task permits it.

Short answers. Clear recording status. Easy repeat and stop.

About 1 minute. Useful means understandable, relevant, and usable in the current task.

Why “when the task permits it”? Safety always takes precedence. Voice assistance is designed for inspection rounds, troubleshooting, and lab reference lookup—never for distracting an operator during safety-critical, high-focus chemical handling or active valve transfers.

A spoken wall of text is not a good voice interface; offer a short answer followed by more detail on request, with references available on screen.

Business Value: Where Does Voice Help? 💰

Persona Potential use case Value to measure
Lab technician Retrieve a method or reference Fewer trips to a terminal; faster lookup
Maintenance technician Retrieve troubleshooting knowledge Less interruption; faster access to context
Driver Continue working on tasks when not in front of the computer Access when typing is impractical

Prioritize by frequency × friction removed × feasibility * value generated.

About 1.5 minutes. Choose a frequent, bounded information-retrieval task with usable source material and permitted device use. Do not assume all labs have lower barriers than all plant areas. Observe users and establish the current workflow before selecting the pilot. Drivers need a different activation and attention model; do not ask them to operate a phone PTT button while driving. Buy speech capabilities and approved equipment; build the CoVA integration and environment-specific UX.

Business Value: Make It Measurable 📐

What improves when the same CoVA becomes accessible by voice?

100 users × 3 queries/day × 1 min saved = ~1,100 hrs/year

  • Direct benefit: Hands-busy lookup without stopping tasks or walking to terminals.
  • Strategic lever: Faster anomaly resolution avoids costly line stoppages.
  • Validation gate: Measure actual time saved vs. baseline workflow before expanding.

About 1.5 minutes. This is an illustrative calculation, not a Covestro forecast. Validate successful usage, baseline time, and net time saved including retries. Capacity released is not automatically cash savings. Subtract implementation, equipment, speech/model inference, hosting, and support costs when assessing economics. MTTR and first-time-fix rate are downstream measures for maintenance expansion, not universal KPIs or benefits attributable to voice without evidence. Avoid transferring vendor AI-maintenance benchmarks to this interface.

KPIs: Are We on Track? 📊

Measure Definition
Adoption Weekly active users / eligible pilot users
Task success Usable information without switching channel
Latency p50/p95: end of speech → first useful spoken response
Domain accuracy Correct chemicals, equipment IDs, numbers, and units
Business impact Task time compared with the baseline workflow

Proposed pilot latency target: p95 ≤ 3 seconds in agreed test conditions.

About 2 minutes. Target is a hypothesis to agree with users, not an industry benchmark.

Latency budget justification (p95 ≤ 3s): - Human conversational psychology: If latency exceeds 2–3s, users assume the system failed and press PTT again, causing duplicate queries. - Component breakdown: Network uplink/downlink (200-400ms) + STT finalization (300-600ms) + CoVA RAG & LLM response (1200-1800ms) + TTS audio chunk synthesis (200-400ms) = ~1.9s to 3.2s. Median (p50) sits ~2s; p95 under 3s keeps user trust high.

Evaluate meaningful response latency, not a filler phrase such as ‘Let me check’. For full-duplex, annotate utterance end consistently and measure interruption-to-playback-stop separately. Track return usage, clarification, retries, mode changes, and user feedback as diagnostic measures. Domain accuracy should use an expert-labelled test set; overall WER alone hides important entity errors. Define task-specific quality gates with domain experts before field expansion; do not invent a universal accuracy threshold.

Options: How Do We Get There? 🔀

Option Interaction and processing What do we learn?
KISS PTT → cloud STT → CoVA → TTS Can voice deliver value quickly?
Hybrid Local STT/TTS + CoVA over a text connection Does reduced bandwidth help?
Full-duplex Streaming speech-to-speech + CoVA integration Does natural conversation help?

Processing location and conversation style are separate decisions.

About 2 minutes. STT means speech-to-text; TTS means text-to-speech; PTT means push-to-talk. Cloud Transcribe/Polly are the turn-based baseline candidates. Local models such as Whisper are candidates to benchmark, not a settled mobile solution. Nova 2 Sonic is the full-duplex candidate. Hybrid can be an environment-specific mode rather than an automatic fallback on every latency spike. Compare on identical tasks and devices.

Solutions: Architecture & Design 👩🏻‍🔧

Architecture: KISS / Hybrid 👩🏻‍🔧

About 2 minutes. Both modes use the same CoVA answer and permissions. The diagram is logical: API Gateway/Lambda can implement bounded turn-based requests; establish audio/request limits and use an asynchronous path if processing exceeds the chosen integration timeout. Device credentials authenticate access, but the backend must validate the user session and enforce CoVA permissions. Preserve text and source references. Use service-compatible audio formats; codec selection is a measured implementation detail. Hybrid needs a text connection to CoVA.

Architecture: Full-duplex 💬

About 2 minutes. Use InvokeModelWithBidirectionalStream and adapt the AWS TypeScript sample [1].

Interruption handling (explain verbally): Nova Sonic natively handles interruptions. When the worker speaks while audio is playing, new audio events sent via WebSocket trigger an interruption event from Sonic back to the Session Service, causing the UI to immediately cancel audio playback.

Full-duplex adds a conversational model around CoVA. Configure information requests to use CoVA and evaluate whether Sonic faithfully preserves tool results, especially numbers, units, and instructions. Do not assume a prompt guarantees this. Keep acknowledgements distinct from substantive answers; require the tool result before answering a company-knowledge question. The session service is persistent compute, not an ordinary Lambda invocation per audio chunk. Hosting is a separate decision on the Deployment slide.

Accuracy, latency, battery, and cost: benchmark rather than assume.

About 1 minute. Local STT is not inherently less accurate, and cloud STT is not automatically best for chemical jargon. Results depend on model, vocabulary support, microphone, accent, and noise. Local battery cost depends on actual duty cycle, not continuous inference for an entire shift. Local model distribution can be separate from app updates. Compare candidate runtimes on the supported Android/iOS devices before choosing one.

Dealing with Chemical Jargon 🧪

  • Curated test set: Chemical formulas, trade names, equipment IDs, and units.
  • Custom vocabulary: Domain biasing on STT where supported (e.g. Transcribe).
  • Knowledge grounding: CoVA RAG context to disambiguate terms and prevent hallucinations.
  • Audio verification: Validate phonetic clarity and fidelity of synthesized answers.

About 1 minute. Recognition and knowledge grounding solve different problems: retrieving the right document cannot reliably fix an incorrectly recognized chemical name. Amazon Transcribe supports custom vocabularies [2]; do not claim the same API exists for Sonic. Use recordings with representative accents, noise, and PPE, plus held-out evaluation data. Confidence-based confirmation is an option only where appropriate scores are exposed and calibrated. A universal 0.85 threshold is not justified. Evaluate whether spoken acronyms, decimal values, and units are understandable to listeners.

Handling Noise & PPE 🎧

  • Input: Suitable microphone and noise suppression.
  • Output: Intelligible speech with required hearing protection.
  • Controls: Glove-compatible activation, repeat/stop, clear recording status.
  • Validation: Actual phone + headset + PPE, during representative tasks.

About 1 minute. Hardware is part of the solution, not an accessory.

What does “intelligible” mean here? Audible ≠ intelligible. High plant noise combined with hearing protection (earplugs/ear defenders attenuating 25–30 dB) turns soft consonants into muffled sound. If CoVA says “fifteen” and the worker hears “fifty”, or confuses “sulfate” with “sulfite”, it creates severe safety hazards. Intelligible speech requires dynamic range compression, EQ peaking in the speech clarity band (1–3 kHz), and deliberate pacing.

Test intelligibility without compromising awareness of alarms or colleagues. A headset’s PTT button may not be exposed to browser code; validate Bluetooth controls, browser lifecycle, microphone permissions, and background behavior. If the PWA cannot support the required hardware interaction, assess a native bridge. Equipment approval applies to the complete phone/headset/accessory setup in the target zone, not just an EX-labelled headset. Start with existing devices where permitted.

Connectivity: Weak Is Not Offline 📶

  • Good connection: Test cloud speech and full-duplex.
  • Low bandwidth: Local STT/TTS may keep text-based access usable.
  • No connection: Clearly indicate that live CoVA is unavailable.

Change modes explicitly. Avoid silently losing or replaying a question.

About 1 minute. Measure connection setup, packet loss, drops, and reconnection, not just bandwidth. A fallback based on one two-second delay is too simplistic; use observed network behavior and visible user control. Cancel stale requests and prevent duplicate turns after retry. Text display is an option only when eyes and equipment permit it. A limited cache of approved information would be a separate versioned capability; local STT alone does not provide offline knowledge access. Geolocation may suggest a mode but is not proof of device permission and should not automatically start recording.

Deployment, Rollout & Monitoring 🚀👷🏻

Deployment: Build on the Existing Stack 🚀

  • Frontend: Angular voice UI / PWA, delivered via S3 + CloudFront.
  • Turn-based API: API Gateway + Lambda; Transcribe/Polly and CoVA.
  • Full-duplex: Persistent TypeScript-based voice session service; AWS Nova 2 Sonic via Bedrock.
  • Delivery: AWS CDK + GitHub Actions; pilot feature flags and rollback.

Hosting candidates for the full-duplex session service:

  • AWS Lambda MicroVMs: Dedicated WebSocket endpoint per session, fast snapshot start, suspends when idle (ideal for intermittent pilot queries; zero idle compute).
  • AWS ECS / Fargate: Shared container pool behind an ALB (ideal for production scale with high, sustained concurrency).

About 2 minutes. Propose testing MicroVMs first for the intermittent pilot, subject to region availability and IaC support, rather than asserting they are faster or cheaper. These are distinct from ordinary Lambda functions. A backend session broker authenticates the user, launches a MicroVM, and returns its endpoint plus a short-lived port-scoped JWE token. Browser WebSockets can pass MicroVM authentication via subprotocols [3]. That token grants endpoint access, not CoVA permissions. MicroVMs have an eight-hour maximum lifespan including suspension, no load balancing between their dedicated endpoints, and snapshot/storage charges while suspended [4]. Keep them running during active audio; resume hooks must refresh credentials and reconnect to Bedrock. Sonic has a separate eight-minute connection limit and renewal pattern [5]. Fargate may be more efficient when many sessions share warm tasks. Compare full session-ready latency, concurrent session capacity, recovery, and cost per successful task. Ordinary Lambda response streaming is not itself a bidirectional WebSocket server; API Gateway WebSocket integrations retain their own limits [6]. Deploy to staging, run regression evaluation, then use feature flags/canary cohorts with a quick rollback path. Use GitHub OIDC for deployment credentials.

Rollout: Get to the Next Phase 👷🏻

Phase What we validate Exit evidence
Experiment Jargon, devices, and network profiles Known failures understood; quality gates agreed
Pilot One frequent, bounded use case Usable answers, acceptable latency, workflow benefit
Expansion Noise, PPE, connectivity, equipment Target setup works during representative tasks
Scale More sites, personas, and languages Repeatable quality, support, and economics

Proposed evaluation window: 6–12 weeks, not a promise of global production.

About 2 minutes. Recruit representative users with operations, domain experts, and relevant local stakeholders. Start with a permitted environment and a bounded use case, not an arbitrary headcount. Give users a short onboarding session and a clear feedback/support route. For lab users, focus on glove-compatible lookup; for plant users, equipment approval and noise; for drivers, brief audio and suitable controls where permitted. Compare modes on the same tasks rather than different populations alone. Local-language validation is a later expansion gate, even if a model technically supports the language. Name a product owner and operational owner before scaling.

Monitoring: What Breaks? 🔎

  • Operations: Latency, failed sessions, connection drops, cost per successful task.
  • Quality: Domain errors, clarification, and task failure by mode and environment.
  • Feedback: Weekly review with users and domain experts.
  • Improvement: Versioned evaluation before changing models, vocabulary, or prompts.

About 1.5 minutes. CloudWatch dashboards and correlated session traces across voice and CoVA. Separate capture, transcription, retrieval/answer, streaming, and playback failures. Alert on service degradation and disable a problematic mode if needed. Track model, vocabulary, prompt, device, and client versions to identify regressions. Do not interpret every clarification as failure; the correct question can prevent a wrong answer. Use selected, authorised diagnostic recordings with defined retention rather than logging all audio by default. Feed knowledge failures back to the CoVA team and audio/UX failures to the voice team.

Limitations & Recommendation 🧐📝

Limitations 🧐

  • No connectivity: No (live) CoVA answer.
  • Some environments may never support an approved, usable voice setup.
  • English-first validates the technology, not global adoption.

About 1 minute. Equipment cost, battery life, model quality, and operational acceptance remain empirical questions. This assistant is an information-access channel; it is not a substitute for required procedures or emergency systems. Full-duplex spoken-answer fidelity is an additional evaluation concern. Avoid claiming that model support for a language proves usability for a local workforce. Transition: given these limits, here is the next decision to make.

Recommendation: One CoVA, with voice modes that fit the job 📝

  1. Build a KISS baseline on the existing CoVA stack.
  2. Validate jargon, audio usability, and workflow value with representative users.
  3. Compare hybrid and full-duplex against the same tasks over 6–12 weeks.
  4. Expand by environment when the evidence supports it.

About 1.5 minutes. Ask for a bounded pilot with an operational partner and access to representative tasks, devices, and source material. The outcome is evidence for which modes and equipment to productionize, with an owner and support path. We can keep one shared CoVA integration and evaluation framework while supporting multiple voice experiences. Do not promise convergence to one mode or operating every option indefinitely.

Q&A

Thank you 🙏🏻

Likely questions & panel challenges:

  • Why Nova 2 Sonic vs. OpenAI Realtime / Gemini Live? Data residency, existing AWS CDK stack, IAM perimeter, and enterprise Bedrock tenant. Keeps data strictly inside Covestro. The session relay architecture isolates the model provider—if Sonic underperforms, we can swap backends without frontend changes.
  • PWA vs. Native Mobile with hardware PTT buttons? Web Bluetooth/Audio has limits when backgrounded or locked. Lab pilot uses active screens. For pocketed plant phones, bench testing will determine if a lightweight native wrapper (Capacitor/Kotlin) is needed for background hardware PTT hooks.
  • RAG tool latency in full-duplex (Sonic waiting for CoVA)? Sonic supports conversational prosody, but artificial filler phrases annoy operators. The session service sends an audible earcon/status cue so the user knows CoVA is retrieving verified facts, avoiding ungrounded speech.
  • High EX headset costs vs. small pilot scale? In chemical operations, ROI is driven by avoiding unplanned downtime (€50k–€100k+ per stoppage) and compliance errors, not typing speed. Preventing one incident pays for the program.
  • Authentication without typing? Reuse CoVA/Entra sign-in on managed devices, within session policy. MDM alone does not authenticate a person. A MicroVM token is a separate endpoint credential.
  • Why not local STT everywhere? Benchmark it; feasibility, duty-cycle battery use, jargon, and supported device runtimes determine whether it wins.
  • Why MicroVMs? Dedicated WebSocket ingress and on-demand lifecycle are useful for intermittent sessions. Compare with pooled Fargate capacity; do not assume lower cost or latency.
  • What happens after a connection drop? Show status, cancel stale turns, reconnect and renew the Sonic stream, and restore context explicitly. Do not silently replay a question.
  • What if the answer is wrong? Keep CoVA’s grounding and authorization, confirm ambiguous entities, and evaluate spoken output separately. Grounding is not a guarantee against hallucination.
  • Make or buy? Use managed speech services and approved commercial equipment. Build the voice-to-CoVA adapter, user experience, and evaluation. Broader platform replacement is unnecessary for this scope.
  • Cost at scale? Measure speech duration, CoVA/tool inference, concurrency, hosting, snapshots/networking, equipment, and support. Estimate cost per successful task with the selected region’s current prices.
